Brinjal Pest and Disease Management — Northeast India

Brinjal Shoot and Fruit Borer (Leucinodes orbonalis)
The single most damaging pest of brinjal in Assam and all of Northeast India. Can cause up to 70–80% yield loss if unmanaged.

Symptoms:
- Young larvae bore into tender shoots causing wilting and drooping of shoot tips (dead heart)
- Larvae enter fruits leaving small entry holes; fruit shows premature ripening and rotting
- Infested fruits unsaleable

Life Cycle:
- Active year-round in Assam; peak infestation June–October (monsoon season)
- Moth lays eggs on undersides of leaves; larvae bore in within 2–3 days

Management (Integrated approach):
1. Remove and destroy wilted shoots (dead hearts) weekly — critical cultural practice
2. Install pheromone traps: 5 traps per acre for monitoring; replace lure every 4–6 weeks
3. Spray Chlorantraniliprole 18.5 SC at 0.5 ml per liter — most effective
4. Alternatively: Spinosad 45 SC at 0.5 ml per liter every 10–14 days
5. Emamectin benzoate 5 SG at 0.4g per liter — good systemic action
6. Do not spray same chemical continuously — rotate chemical groups
7. Harvest frequently (every 3–4 days) to remove infested fruits before larvae emerge
8. Pre-harvest interval: Chlorantraniliprole 3 days; Spinosad 1 day; Emamectin 3 days
9. Biological control: Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) spray at larval emergence effective in low-pressure situations
10. Barrier crop: Plant marigold rows around brinjal fields to attract adult moths

Brinjal Jassid/Leafhopper (Amrasca biguttula)
Common in dry hot weather conditions — May to July.
Symptoms: Leaf curl, yellowing, stunting; "hopper burn" on leaf margins
Management: Spray Imidacloprid 17.8 SL at 0.5 ml/liter or Thiamethoxam 25 WG at 0.2g/liter

Aphids (Aphis gossypii)
Attack young shoots and underside of leaves; transmit viruses.
Management: Dimethoate 30 EC at 2 ml/liter; Encourage natural enemies (ladybird beetles)

Red Spider Mite (Tetranychus urticae)
Severe in dry hot periods (March–May) and during dry breaks in monsoon.
Symptoms: Yellowing and bronzing; fine webbing on leaf undersides
Management: Abamectin 1.9 EC at 0.5 ml/liter; Maintain moisture; Sulphur dust in early stages

Whitefly (Bemisia tabaci)
Transmits brinjal yellow mosaic virus (major concern).
Management: Yellow sticky traps; Spiromesifen 240 SC at 1 ml/liter; Silver mulch repels

Brinjal Diseases in Assam

Phomopsis Blight / Fruit Rot (Phomopsis vexans)
Most common disease of brinjal in Assam — favored by warm humid conditions.
Symptoms: Brown circular spots on leaves; sunken dark spots on fruits; seedling damping-off
Management:
- Seed treatment: Thiram 3g per kg seed before sowing
- Spray Mancozeb 75 WP at 2.5g/liter at 10-day intervals
- Copper oxychloride 3g/liter as protectant spray during monsoon
- Remove and destroy infected fruits immediately

Bacterial Wilt (Ralstonia solanacearum)
Serious problem in Assam, especially in warm wet conditions.
Symptoms: Sudden wilting of entire plant; no yellowing; white bacterial ooze from cut stem in water
No cure once infected.
Management:
- Crop rotation: avoid brinjal or other solanaceous crops for 3 years in affected field
- Soil solarization: cover moist soil with clear polythene for 6–8 weeks in summer
- Remove and burn infected plants immediately — do not compost
- Grafting: graft brinjal onto Solanum torvum (wild brinjal) rootstock — highly resistant
- Trichoderma viride soil application at transplanting as biocontrol

Little Leaf Disease (Phytoplasma)
Important viral disease in Assam.
Symptoms: Small pale yellow leaves; multiple shoots from axillary buds; stunted plants; no fruit
Transmitted by leafhoppers.
Management:
- Remove and destroy infected plants immediately
- Control leafhopper vector with Imidacloprid 0.5 ml/liter
- No cure for infected plants
- Use resistant varieties: Pusa Purple Cluster, Muktakeshi

Cercospora Leaf Spot
Symptoms: Circular pale brown spots with dark margins on leaves
Management: Mancozeb 75 WP at 2.5g/liter or Carbendazim 1g/liter spray at 14-day intervals

Damping Off (nursery)
Rhizoctonia and Pythium cause seed and seedling rot in nurseries.
Management: Drench nursery bed with Metalaxyl + Mancozeb 2.5g/liter before sowing; Trichoderma viride application

Spray Schedule for Brinjal in Assam (Monsoon Season Kharif Crop)

Week 1 after transplanting: Chlorpyrifos 2 ml/liter (soil pest and cutworm)
Week 3: Mancozeb 2.5g/liter (fungal disease preventive)
Week 5: Spinosad 0.5 ml/liter (shoot borer)
Week 7: Copper oxychloride 3g/liter (bacterial + fungal diseases)
Week 9: Chlorantraniliprole 0.5 ml/liter (fruit borer)
Week 11: Abamectin 0.5 ml/liter (mites + fruit borer)
Continue rotating chemicals at 10–14 day intervals throughout harvest season.

Note: Always observe pre-harvest interval (PHI). Wash fruits thoroughly before consumption.
