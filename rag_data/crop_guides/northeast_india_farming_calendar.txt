Northeast India Farming Calendar — Assam, Meghalaya, Nagaland, Manipur

Agro-Climatic Zones of Northeast India
Northeast India has three main agro-climatic zones:
1. Brahmaputra Valley (Assam plains) — humid subtropical, hot summers, warm winters
2. Barak Valley (south Assam) — similar to plains but slightly drier
3. Hill regions (Karbi Anglong, NC Hills, Meghalaya, Nagaland plateau) — cool to cold, high rainfall

Annual Rainfall: 1800–3000 mm (Brahmaputra valley); up to 11,000 mm in Cherrapunji
Monsoon: June to September (Southwest monsoon dominates)
Winter: December to February (dry, cool; occasional frost in hills above 800 m)

Monthly Farming Calendar for Assam Plains

January
- Harvest: Rabi vegetables (cabbage, cauliflower, potato, pea, carrot)
- Harvest: Mustard if sown in October
- Maintenance: Irrigation for standing rabi crops
- Sowing: Early summer vegetables (brinjal, tomato nursery for summer crop)
- Apply fertilizer top-dress to winter crops

February
- Harvest: Continue rabi vegetable harvest
- Nursery: Raise brinjal, tomato, chili seedlings for summer planting
- Land preparation: Summer field crops
- Lemon/citrus: Apply fertilizer and first irrigation of the year
- Important: Wheat harvest in upper Assam if cultivated

March
- Transplant: Brinjal, tomato, chili summer crop seedlings
- Sow: Cucumber, bottle gourd, bitter gourd directly
- Harvesting: Late rabi crops
- Land preparation: Summer paddy (Boro paddy) finishing
- Fertilizer application to transplanted summer vegetables

April
- Brinjal/tomato summer crop: tending, irrigation, staking
- Harvesting: Boro paddy beginning
- Sow: Okra, moong bean, sesame
- Pest monitoring: Fruit borer in tomato and brinjal increases
- Pre-monsoon land preparation

May
- Brinjal/tomato: Peak harvest (summer crop)
- Paddy: Nursery raising for Sali (main Kharif) paddy
- Land preparation: Sali paddy fields
- Vegetable: Harvest cucumber, bottle gourd
- Spray: Pre-monsoon pest management for citrus

June (Monsoon begins)
- Paddy transplanting: Sali paddy (main crop of Assam)
- Brinjal nursery: Raise Kharif brinjal nursery under shade/polytunnel
- Harvesting: Summer vegetable crops ending
- Drainage: Ensure all vegetable fields have drainage channels
- Important: Reduce nitrogen applications during peak monsoon

July
- Sali paddy: Transplanting and tending
- Brinjal: Transplant Kharif nursery seedlings (raised beds essential)
- Vegetable management: Fungal disease pressure high — preventive sprays
- Ginger/turmeric: Peak growth, earthing up, weed management
- Hill farmers: Maize harvest begins

August
- Paddy: Vegetative to reproductive phase
- Brinjal: First harvest of Kharif crop begins
- Pest alert: Brinjal shoot and fruit borer peak season
- Cabbage nursery: Raise seedlings for Rabi season
- Ginger: Pseudostem borer monitoring

September (Late Monsoon)
- Paddy: Flowering and grain filling
- Brinjal: Peak Kharif harvest — market prices typically lowest
- Transplant: Cabbage, cauliflower, potato (early varieties)
- Land preparation: Rabi season begins for plains farmers
- Harvest: Early maize

October
- Paddy: Sali paddy harvest begins in early-maturing areas
- Vegetable transplanting: Cabbage, cauliflower, tomato Rabi crop
- Potato: Planting begins in Assam plains
- Brinjal: Kharif crop ending, Rabi crop being transplanted
- Mustard: Sowing begins
- Important: Post-paddy field preparation for winter vegetables

November
- Paddy: Sali paddy main harvest — most important month
- Vegetables: Rabi crop establishment — tomato, brinjal, cabbage tending
- Potato: Earth up and weed management
- Mustard: Growing stage
- Lemon: Harvest begins for main crop

December
- Vegetable harvest: Cabbage, cauliflower, early potato
- Brinjal Rabi: Peak harvest — best prices of the year
- Mustard: Flowering stage
- Cold weather pest management: Aphids active in cool dry weather
- Betel nut (Pan) plantations: Annual maintenance

Key Farming Practices for Assam
1. Paddy is the dominant crop — vegetable cultivation fills gaps in paddy calendar
2. Multiple cropping: Paddy followed by mustard and then vegetables
3. Jhum (shifting cultivation) still practiced in hill districts
4. Betel nut and betel vine are important cash crops alongside vegetables
5. Flood and waterlogging is the biggest risk in Brahmaputra plains — raised bed planting essential

Soil Types in Assam
- Alluvial soils (Brahmaputra valley): very fertile, medium texture, slightly acidic
- Lateritic soils (foothills): high in iron, acidic (pH 4.5–5.5), needs liming
- Forest soils (hills): rich in organic matter, very acidic
- Most Assam soils are low in phosphorus, zinc, and sulfur

Water Management
- Brahmaputra valley: flooding risk June–September
- Barak valley: drought-like conditions possible in winter
- Hill areas: terracing essential, check dams for water conservation
- Drip irrigation increasingly adopted for winter vegetable gardens
