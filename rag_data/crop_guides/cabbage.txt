Cabbage Cultivation Guide — Northeast India / Assam

Overview
Cabbage (Brassica oleracea var. capitata) is a major Rabi vegetable crop in Northeast India. Assam, Meghalaya, and Nagaland are significant producers. Cabbage is grown mainly in the cool winter season (October–March) in Assam plains, and year-round in hill districts.

Best Time to Plant Cabbage in Assam

Plains (Brahmaputra Valley):
- Nursery sowing: August to September
- Transplanting: September to October
- Harvest: December to February
- This is the main cabbage season in Assam

Hill Districts (Karbi Anglong, Dima Hasao):
- Nursery sowing: February to March (spring crop) or June to July (summer crop)
- Transplanting: March to April or July to August
- Harvest: May to June or October to November
- Hills allow extended cabbage season due to cooler temperatures

Climate Requirements
- Optimum temperature for head formation: 15°C to 20°C
- Cabbage requires cool weather — high temperatures cause bolting and loose heads
- In Assam plains, plant only in cool months (September–November transplanting)
- Tolerates light frost after head initiation
- Not suitable for hot humid summer months in Brahmaputra valley

Recommended Varieties for Assam
1. Golden Acre — early variety (55–60 days), small compact heads, very popular
2. Pride of India — medium duration, good for Assam plains
3. Pusa Drum Head — late variety, large heads, high yield
4. Quisto — hybrid, uniform heads, bolt-resistant, excellent for Assam hills
5. Green Express — hybrid, early maturity 50–55 days, tight heads
6. Local varieties: Bodo varieties preferred in specific districts

Nursery Preparation
- Prepare raised nursery bed 15–20 cm high to ensure drainage
- Mix soil, FYM, and sand in equal proportions
- Drench bed with Copper oxychloride 3g per liter before sowing
- Sow seeds 2–3 cm apart in rows 10 cm apart
- Transplant 25–30 days after sowing when seedlings are 12–15 cm tall
- Harden seedlings by reducing irrigation 5 days before transplanting

Land Preparation and Planting
- Deep plough and apply 25 tonnes FYM per hectare
- Lime if soil pH below 6.0 — apply 1–2 tonnes/ha
- Planting distance: 45×45 cm (small varieties) or 60×60 cm (large varieties)
- Transplant in evening, water immediately

Fertilizer Application (per hectare)
- FYM: 25 tonnes (basal)
- N: 120 kg/ha — apply one-third at planting, rest in two splits at 30 and 45 days
- P₂O₅: 60 kg/ha — full at planting as basal
- K₂O: 60 kg/ha — full at planting as basal
- Boron deficiency common in Assam — apply 1.5 kg borax per hectare or foliar spray 0.2% boron

Irrigation
- Regular irrigation at 10–12 day intervals
- Critical period: head initiation stage — water stress causes small loose heads
- Stop irrigation 1 week before harvest for better keeping quality
- Avoid overhead irrigation once heads begin forming (invites black rot)

Pest and Disease Management
- Diamond-back moth (Plutella xylostella): major pest; spray Spinosad 0.5 ml/liter
- Aphids: spray Dimethoate 2 ml/liter or use yellow sticky traps
- Black rot (Xanthomonas campestris): use disease-free seeds; copper fungicide spray
- Damping off in nursery: drench with Trichoderma viride solution

Harvesting
- Harvest when head is firm and compact — press with palm; firm head bounces back
- Cut at base leaving 2–3 outer leaves as protection
- Yield: 25–40 tonnes per hectare depending on variety and management
- Peak market price: December to January in Assam

Post-Harvest
- Do not remove outer wrapper leaves until sale
- Store at 0–4°C for 4–6 weeks
- For local Assam markets: pack in bamboo baskets or gunny bags
- Transport carefully to avoid head damage — bruising causes rapid decay
