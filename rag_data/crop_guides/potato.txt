Potato Cultivation Guide — Northeast India (ICAR)

Climate Requirements
Potato requires cool weather with temperature 15–20°C during tuber formation. In Northeast India, potato is grown from October to February in plains (Assam, Tripura) and from March to June in hills (Meghalaya, Nagaland). High humidity above 90% promotes late blight — a major threat in the region.

Seed Selection and Treatment
Use certified disease-free seed tubers. Recommended varieties: Kufri Jyoti, Kufri Sindhuri (resistant to late blight), Kufri Himalini for hills. Cut tubers to 30–40g pieces with at least 2 eyes. Treat cut pieces with Mancozeb 0.25% solution for 30 minutes before planting to prevent seed-borne diseases.

Planting
Plant tubers at 5–7 cm depth, 60×20 cm spacing. Plant population: 40,000–50,000 plants per hectare. Use well-decomposed farmyard manure at 20 tonnes per hectare. Apply lime if soil pH is below 5.5 — target pH 5.0 to 6.5 for potato.

Fertilizer Application
Apply NPK at 150:80:100 kg per hectare. Apply full P, K, and half N as basal. Apply remaining N in 2 splits at earthing-up (30 days) and 50 days after planting. Deficiency of potassium causes hollow heart in tubers — ensure adequate K application.

Earthing-Up
Perform first earthing-up at 25–30 days when plants are 15–20 cm tall. Second earthing-up at 45 days. Proper earthing-up prevents tuber greening (solanine formation) and improves drainage.

Disease Management
Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans) — most serious disease in Northeast India. Apply Metalaxyl + Mancozeb (Ridomil Gold) at first appearance. Spray at 7-day intervals during cool humid weather. Remove and destroy infected haulms immediately. Bacterial Wilt — use crop rotation with non-solanaceous crops. Early Blight — apply Chlorothalonil or Mancozeb preventively.

Harvesting
Harvest 75–120 days after planting depending on variety. Harvest when 75% of haulms have dried. Avoid harvesting in wet conditions. Allow tubers to cure at 15–18°C for 10–14 days before storage. Yield: 20–30 tonnes per hectare.

Storage
Store in cool, dark, ventilated conditions at 4°C for long-term. For farm-level storage in Northeast India, use earthen cool chambers or evaporative cooling stores. Avoid exposure to light — causes greening and solanine buildup which is toxic.
