Ginger and Turmeric Cultivation Guide — Northeast India (ICAR)

Ginger (Zingiber officinale)

Importance in Northeast India
Northeast India, especially Meghalaya (Nadia, Thadlaskein), Assam, and Mizoram, accounts for over 20% of India's ginger production. Meghalaya ginger is known for its high oleoresin content and pungency.

Climate and Soil
Ginger requires 1500–3000 mm annual rainfall with high humidity 75–90%. Temperature range 20–30°C. Grows well in partially shaded conditions — intercrop with areca nut or under forest canopy. Well-drained red laterite soils with pH 5.5–6.5 preferred. Avoid waterlogging — causes rhizome rot.

Planting Material
Use disease-free healthy rhizomes of 20–25g each with 2–3 buds. Sett treatment: soak in Mancozeb 0.3% + Carbendazim 0.1% solution for 30 minutes. Recommended varieties: Nadia (Meghalaya), Maran, Tafengma, Himachal.

Planting Time and Spacing
Plant April–May at onset of monsoon. Spacing: 30×20 cm, depth 5 cm. Mulch immediately with green leaves at 10 tonnes per hectare. Apply mulch 3 times during season. Mulching conserves moisture, reduces weed growth, and adds organic matter.

Fertilizer
Apply 30 tonnes FYM at land preparation. NPK: 75:75:75 kg per hectare. Split N into 3 doses (planting, 60 days, 90 days). Spray 2% KNO3 at 60 and 90 days after planting to improve rhizome quality.

Harvesting
Green ginger harvest at 6 months (180 days). Dry ginger at 8–9 months when leaves turn yellow. Yield: fresh ginger 15–20 tonnes per hectare; dry ginger 2–3 tonnes per hectare. Northeast India ginger fetches premium price in national and export markets.

Turmeric (Curcuma longa)

Overview
Assam, Meghalaya, and Manipur are major producers. Manipuri turmeric (locally called 'Umorok Haldi') has high curcumin content 5–7% versus national average of 2–3%.

Climate and Soil
Temperature 20–35°C. Annual rainfall 1500–2500 mm. Thrives in humid tropical to subtropical conditions. Rich loamy to clayey loam soils with pH 4.5–7.5.

Planting
Plant May–June. Use rhizomes 40–60g (mother or finger rhizomes). Spacing: 45×30 cm. Mulch at planting with paddy straw or green leaves. Earth up at 60 and 120 days.

Fertilizer
FYM 30 tonnes per hectare. NPK 60:50:120 kg per hectare. Higher K improves curcumin content. Split N into 3 equal doses.

Disease Management
Rhizome rot (Pythium species) — ensure good drainage, treat planting material with Metalaxyl. Leaf blotch (Taphrina maculans) — spray Mancozeb 0.25%. Nematodes — apply Carbofuran 3G at planting.

Harvesting
Harvest at 7–9 months when leaves dry. Cure boiled turmeric at 60–65°C for uniform color. Polished turmeric gives better market price. Yield: 20–25 tonnes fresh, 4–5 tonnes dry per hectare.
