Brinjal (Eggplant) Cultivation Guide — Northeast India / Assam (ICAR)

Overview
Brinjal (Solanum melongena), locally known as begun in Assam, is the most important vegetable crop in Northeast India after tomato. It is grown year-round in Assam's plains and foothill regions. Assam is a major producer of brinjal in India, with varieties adapted to the humid subtropical climate.

Best Time to Plant Brinjal in Assam
Assam has two main brinjal planting seasons:

Kharif (Summer-Rainy) Season:
- Nursery sowing: February to March
- Transplanting: March to April
- Harvest: June to October
- Suitable for: plains and lower foothills of Assam

Rabi (Winter) Season:
- Nursery sowing: June to August
- Transplanting: July to September
- Harvest: October to February
- Suitable for: all regions of Assam including Brahmaputra valley

Year-round cultivation is possible in Assam's warm lowlands. The Rabi season crop is generally more profitable due to fewer pest problems and better market prices.

Climate Requirements
- Optimum temperature: 22°C to 32°C
- Brinjal is sensitive to frost — avoid planting in December-January in upper Assam hills
- Relative humidity: 60–70% (higher humidity encourages fungal disease)
- Rainfall: 600–1200 mm per season
- Assam's June-September monsoon can cause waterlogging — ensure proper drainage

Recommended Varieties for Assam
1. Pusa Purple Long — most popular, high yield, good for Brahmaputra valley
2. Pusa Purple Cluster — suitable for all regions, resistant to little leaf disease
3. Muktakeshi — long purple, very popular in Assam markets, good shelf life
4. Assam Local Long — traditional variety, adapted to local conditions
5. Hybrid varieties: Arka Neelkanth, Pusa Hybrid-6 — higher yield but more input-intensive
6. Swarna Shyamala — resistant to bacterial wilt, suited for humid areas

Nursery Preparation
- Prepare raised nursery beds: 1 m wide, 3 m long, 20 cm high
- Mix soil with well-rotted farmyard manure and sand (2:1:1 ratio)
- Treat nursery bed with Bavistin 2g per liter or Captan 3g per liter (drench) before sowing
- Sow seeds at 3–4 cm spacing, cover lightly with fine soil
- Provide shade with 50% shade net during summer nursery raising
- Seeds germinate in 7–10 days
- Seedlings ready for transplanting in 25–30 days when 10–15 cm tall with 4–5 leaves

Land Preparation
- Deep plough 30–45 cm for better root development
- Apply 20–25 tonnes of well-rotted farmyard manure (FYM) per hectare
- Final ploughing: bring to fine tilth, make raised beds to avoid waterlogging
- Recommended spacing: 60×60 cm or 75×60 cm
- Ridge and furrow system recommended in waterlogged areas of Assam

Fertilizer Schedule (per hectare)
Basal dose (at transplanting):
- FYM: 20–25 tonnes
- N: 50 kg (as urea)
- P₂O₅: 50 kg (as SSP)
- K₂O: 50 kg (as MOP)

Top dressing:
- First: 30 days after transplanting — 50 kg N
- Second: 60 days after transplanting — 50 kg N
Total N: 150 kg/ha, P: 50 kg/ha, K: 50 kg/ha

Micronutrient: Foliar spray of 0.5% ZnSO₄ + 0.2% boron at flowering improves fruit set in Assam's zinc-deficient soils.

Irrigation
- Transplant immediately after irrigation
- Irrigation interval: 7–10 days in dry season, reduce during monsoon
- Critical stages: transplanting, flowering, and fruit development
- Avoid waterlogging — raised bed or ridge-furrow planting essential during Assam monsoon
- Drip irrigation recommended for winter crop

Harvesting
- First harvest: 60–75 days after transplanting
- Harvest brinjal when fruits are fully grown but before seeds harden (glossy skin stage)
- Harvest every 4–5 days for fresh market supply
- Use sharp knife or scissors to cut fruit with 2 cm stalk
- Yield: 25–30 tonnes per hectare for open-pollinated varieties, 35–50 t/ha for hybrids

Post-Harvest and Marketing
- Grade by size and color — no bruising or disease marks
- Pack in ventilated bamboo baskets or crates (traditional Assam market packaging)
- Shelf life at ambient temperature: 3–5 days; at 10°C: 1–2 weeks
- Best selling markets: Fancy Bazar (Guwahati), Paltan Bazar, district level APMC yards
- Peak prices: November to February (Rabi crop, less supply)
